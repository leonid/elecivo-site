Getting started with Elecivo

From a floor plan to a priced, documented electrical installation — and the site work that follows. About 15 minutes to read.

1. The idea

You describe the installation once — rooms, devices and circuits — and everything else is derived from it: cable lengths from the routes, voltage drop and cable capacity, the distribution board, the bill of materials, labour, the quotation and every drawing. Change a device and all of them follow. Nothing in the documents is typed by hand.

Two projects come with the app: Demo, a made-up apartment to try things on, and Pilot, a real apartment. Open the project panel with the project name at the top of the menu.

The menu groups the screens the way the job runs: Design (plan, rooms and devices, circuits, board), Quote (bill of materials, documents) and Site (work log). Every screen has its own address: bookmark it, open it in a new tab, or send it to someone who has the project.

The app works without an account and without a connection: everything is kept in this browser. An account adds sync between devices, sharing with your team and backups on the server.

2. Projects and revisions

The app opens on Projects: your jobs on this device, each with where it stands (in design or on site, unsaved changes, the last revision), and the examples. Click one to open it; Site work opens a crewed job straight on its work log. Projects at the start of the top bar brings you back. On a phone, a job with a crew opens on its work log.

To start your own job, click New project there (or in the project panel): give it a name and choose the country, the supply (single- or three-phase) and the earthing system. It opens on an empty plan; add the rooms, then the devices. A device that fits no existing circuit gets a new one of its own (lighting, sockets or a dedicated appliance circuit), which you can change in Circuits.

Duplicate copies the open project (its last saved revision) to start a similar job — or duplicate the demo to practise on. The copy takes the design, prices and crew, not the work log or material entries. Delete removes a project from this browser; a copy on the server is kept. The demo and the pilot can't be deleted.

Edits are unsaved changes until you save them as a revision. A revision is a full, numbered snapshot (r1, r2, …) that is never changed afterwards — like issuing a drawing.

  1. Make changes anywhere. The top bar shows Unsaved changes; Undo and Redo (Ctrl/⌘+Z, Ctrl/⌘+Shift+Z) work on every edit.
  2. Click Unsaved changes, write a short summary (or leave it empty to use the list of changes) and Save revision.
  3. The project panel lists every revision. Restore loads an older one as unsaved changes; saving it makes it current again — history is never rewritten.

Export project file and Import project file move whole projects between browsers without an account.

3. Drawing the plan

Open Plan and click Edit layout.

Add rooms

Pick + Room and click an empty part of the plan: a 2 × 2 m room appears there, selected, so you can drag its corners to its real shape (a new project's empty plan offers Draw a room for the first one). Name it and set its type under Rooms & devices.

A quick quote, without drawing

For a first price, choose Quick quote when you create the project. In Rooms & devices, add the rooms the way the client describes them (type and area), then Typical devices for … empty rooms: sockets, lights, switches and the fixed appliances appear, on circuits spread over the RCD groups the load needs. The set depends on the room's type and area — e.g. a bedroom gets a socket per 5 m² (at least 3), a light and a switch; a kitchen 4 worktop sockets, an oven with hob and a dishwasher. It is our planning assumption, not a standard: check it with the client and change what differs. In a room already drawn on the plan the devices are laid out inside it — lights across the middle, switches beside the door, the rest round the walls — ready to be dragged where they really go. One undo takes it back.

Cable lengths are then estimated from the size of the flat and the devices on each circuit, shown as ≈ 19 m on Circuits and said to be on the bill of materials and the quote. Type a length you know, or draw the plan later: Place on plan puts a room on it with its devices inside, ready to drag into place; routes then measure the cable.

Trace a drawing

  1. Under Drawing & plan size, load the architect's drawing (PNG, JPG, SVG or PDF).
  2. Use Calibrate: click both ends of a wall whose length you know and enter it. The drawing is scaled to real millimetres.
  3. Add each room with + Room and shape it over the drawing: drag corners, double-click an edge to add one. Room areas follow the outlines.

Rooms you measured yourself

Select a room and type its walls and corner angles in order, as measured; the closing wall is calculated, so a gap there shows a measuring error.

Devices, doors and fixtures

Pick + Socket, + Light, + Switch or + Appliance outlet and click inside a room; the circuit is suggested from the room. Drag to move (10 mm grid), arrow keys to nudge. A selected device's variant picks its accessory: a two-way, intermediate or two-gang switch or a dimmer, a double or IP44 socket, an EV charger. Doors, windows, baths and showers matter too: bathroom zones are checked against them.

Cable routes

First drag the board marker (DB-01) in Select & move to where the board really is: every route starts there. With Cable route, click a device to show its circuit's route; Create routes for all circuits plans them along the walls. Cable lengths then come from the routes, including the drops to each device. In a panel building, whose load-bearing walls may not be chased horizontally, tick Panel building in Project settings: socket and appliance circuits then run in the floor and rise vertically to each device, lighting stays in the ceiling, and a circuit you set back to chased is flagged.

4. Circuits and checks

Circuits lists each circuit with its load, current, cable, breaker, voltage drop and cable capacity. Click a row to edit it in a panel on the right (Esc closes it): the cross-section (1.5 to 16 mm²), the length in metres, the protection and the breaker. The checks run again at once. A new circuit takes its load from the devices you put on it; leave its length empty to have it estimated. Defaults follow the author's one-size-below practice: lighting 1.5 mm² / C10, sockets 2.5 mm² / C16 — IEC 60364 allows C16 on 1.5 mm² (installation method B2), but 10 A leaves a safer margin in use. The breaker curve for new circuits (C curve by default, or B on single-phase / C on three-phase for selectivity behind a small incomer) is set under Distribution board.

Problems appear as issues with a severity (the checks button in the top bar collects them) and, where there is one, a suggested fix. Show on an issue opens what it is about — a circuit in its editor, a room on the plan, an RCD on the board — and the plan and the board mark what has an issue. A warning you judged acceptable can be Accepted with a reason (errors can't): it then counts as settled, and the documents list it with your reason, name and date. If the warning's value later changes, the acceptance lapses and it is open again. Project settings holds what the checks need: supply, earthing, ambient temperature, the earth fault loop impedance Ze at the origin, and the supply protection (the breaker or fuse ahead of the board, in A per phase) — enter the measured or declared values; Elecivo never assumes them. With the supply protection entered, Elecivo also checks the maximum demand of each phase and that every RCD is protected against overload. At the bottom, Appearance picks a colour scheme (Workshop, Panel or Drafting) and light, dark or the device's setting; it is kept in this browser and never changes the project or the printed documents.

Lights and appliances can say what kind of start-up current they draw (motor, LED driver, transformer). Elecivo then warns when the surge may trip the breaker and suggests the next curve (B → C) — but only gives it as a fix when the earth fault loop impedance still allows it. The start-up figures are planning assumptions: check them against the device datasheets.

Each circuit is protected by a breaker in an RCD group or by an RCBO, chosen under Protection. An RCBO has its own 30 mA protection, so a fault on it leaves every other circuit on — useful for fridges, freezers and outdoor circuits; it takes more board space and costs more.

Distribution board shows the enclosure, RCD groups, rail layout, wiring and the single-line diagram, all derived from the circuits. Devices that are not part of the supply → RCD → breaker chain — a contactor, a meter, a timer, a relay — are listed under the circuits on the single-line diagram as Other board devices, each with its designation, rating and what feeds it (not fed while nothing is wired to its input).

Every device on the board carries a reference designation, numbered along the rails from the top left: in Bulgaria QS1 for the main switch, FV1 for the surge protector, QD1, QD2 for the RCDs and QF1, QF2… for the breakers; in Germany the class letter with a minus (-Q1, -F1…). The drawing, the single-line diagram, the labels, the panel legend, the circuit schedule and the test protocol all print it beside the circuit's own code (R7, K-02): the circuit names the line and its loads, the designation the device that protects it. Designations follow the rail order, so moving a device renumbers them; they are never typed. The project's country picks the scheme, which still awaits a designer's confirmation.

To put an RCD group on a particular rail, pick the rail in the DIN rails panel above the rails. The incomer stays first; a choice that does not fit the enclosure is placed automatically and named in a note.

To change which RCD group a circuit sits in, open Edit groups above the board’s group cards and pick the group under each circuit (or a new group). The board, labels and documents follow; the arrangement stays when you add circuits later.

The board design is the devices on the board and the wires between their terminals; it starts as the board the circuits give, and the first change stores it. Once a design has devices, the DIN rails, the enclosure size and the module count follow it: a breaker wired to another RCD moves into that group. The DIN rails view is one drawing of the board: the enclosure, its rails and the wiring ducts between them, every device with its rating, its terminals as dots (inputs on top, outputs at the bottom) and the pale spare modules. It opens in View, the board alone; switch to Edit to change it. The drawing stays where it is in every mode, and − / + above it (or the - + keys, Ctrl / ⌘ + wheel or a trackpad pinch) zoom it, Fit (or 0) returns it to the width of the screen; a zoomed drawing scrolls; the column beside it (below it on a phone) holds what changes: the enclosure, rails and presets while nothing is selected, and the selected device's panel after a click. Every device on the rails has a Type — turn an MCB into an RCBO or a relay into a contactor; its wires stay on the terminals the new type still has, and any it has no terminal for are listed and removed —, its width in Modules for a product wider than the standard (Standard sets it back), its Poles where it comes in more than one (1P, 1P+N, 3P, 3P+N; the width follows) and, on a contactor or relay, its NC contacts, closed while the coil is off. A breaker's Protection offers the two standard solutions, each with a line on when to use it: RCD + MCB in one of the RCD groups (the usual choice: the group shares one 30 mA RCD) or an RCBO with its own 30 mA (a fridge or freezer, an alarm, outdoor or bathroom circuits; it is fed straight from the main switch). Switching keeps the breaker's circuit and its place. Every kind has its standard width — a single-phase surge protector takes 2 modules — and a width set by hand is what the rails, the enclosure and the labour count. Each terminal carries its marking as printed on the device: 1 / 2 (3 / 4, 5 / 6) for the poles, A1 / A2 for a coil, 11 / 12 for an NC contact, 15 / 16 / 18 for a time relay's changeover and 11 / 12 / 14 for an auxiliary contact. Besides that, an RCD's rating, sensitivity and breakers (Add breaker), the circuit a breaker protects, the number of terminals of an N or PE bar, the poles and outlets of a distribution block, the rating of a fuse, whether an energy meter is MID-approved for billing, whether an ammeter is in the line or on a current transformer and its range, a transformer's ratio, one lamp or three (L1 L2 L3), the poles and rating of a switch-disconnector (1P, 1P+N or 3P+N — its width follows), the rating of a changeover switch or impulse relay, a staircase timer's delay, a twilight switch's light level, a thermostat's set point, a load-shedding relay's limit (a new one takes the supply's protection when the project states it), a power supply's output (12 or 24 V DC) and power (its width follows), a bell transformer's voltage, an AFDD's rating, the breaker or switch an auxiliary contact or a shunt trip is clipped to, or Remove. ◀ ▶ or the arrow keys move the selected device one place along the rail (a breaker at the end of its group moves into the next group); Delete removes it. Or drag a device along the rails: an orange line shows where it will go, before or after the device under the pointer. A breaker dropped among another group's breakers joins that group, an RCD takes its whole group with it, and the main switch stays first. The N and PE bars stand beside the rails, coloured blue and green-yellow: one PE bar and one N bar per RCD group are added from the circuits, and they move the same way. Coloured lines in the ducts show what feeds what. Wires (the default) draws every conductor; Groups tints each group's devices, names it under the rail and draws the feeds only. Breakers side by side in one group, fed from the same terminal, share a comb busbar along their tops: it carries their feeds, so only the first breaker's feed is drawn. The board document prints this drawing in its Wires view. To rewire, click a terminal and then the one it should be joined to: the terminals of the same conductor light up, and an input (or a device's PE, or a coil's return) takes its new feed in place of the old one (Esc cancels). Click a wire to select it, then press Delete or Remove wire to remove it. Simulate tries the board out without changing it: Switch the supply on, then live wires are drawn full and dead ones pale, and a lamp on each device shows whether it has power. Click a device to switch it off or on: a changeover switch steps I → 0 → II (its knob shows where it is; II takes the generator input), and a push button is pressed once, turning over the impulse relay it pulses — the relay keeps its contact until the next press, while a staircase timer starts and keeps the light on until you click the timer to end its time. Click a control device to set what it senses — dark or daylight, cold or warm, in or out of the programmed hours, demand over the limit, a phase fault, on or off from the app — and its contact follows (a load-shedding relay drops its load over the limit, a phase monitor opens on a fault or a voltage out of range); an amber lamp in its corner shows it is set; pick a fault (earth leakage, overload, short circuit, arc fault) and where it happens, then Play to see what trips — each event pops up as a notification in the corner for a few seconds (amber when something tripped, red when nothing could clear the fault), and Events keeps the last ones; set the supply voltage too high or too low to see a voltage relay open. Time relays hold off after the supply comes back until you end their delay. Meters show live values on their displays: the voltage as the supply event sets it, and the current and power of the circuits whose breakers are on below them (their design currents); OL means beyond the meter's range. Indicator lamps light while their phase is present. The secondary of a current transformer to its ammeter is drawn as a dotted purple line, and the low-voltage output of a power supply or bell transformer as a dashed orange one. An auxiliary contact feeds its NO output while the device it is clipped to is on and its NC output once it is off or tripped; a shunt trip opens its device as soon as its coil gets 230 V (from a stop button); an AFDD also trips on an arc fault, which a plain breaker does not see. Wiring check lists what is not connected right (a device with no feed or no neutral, a neutral from another RCD, a coil that never closes, an SPD without earth, a breaker with no RCD, a live wired to a neutral, an ammeter not wired to a current transformer, a transformer whose secondary is open, an impulse relay or staircase timer whose coil is fed without a push button, a control device without the supply of its electronics on A1 / A2, a changeover switch with nothing on input II, an auxiliary contact or shunt trip clipped to nothing, a shunt trip whose coil is live without a button) and, where the fix is clear, a button that adds the right wire. In Edit, the palette at the right of the drawing (below it on a tablet or phone) adds a device in one click, wired as usual: Breaker 1P, 1P+N (and 3P, 3P+N on three phases) joins the RCD group of the selected device, or the last group, and the BOM buys it with those poles; RCD is fed from the incomer; RCBO and Surge protector sit right after the incomer; then Voltage relay, Contactor, DIN socket, N bar and PE bar. Add device… holds everything else by section — distribution (an always-on group, a fuse holder, a distribution block, and Main distribution, which puts one block after the main switch and moves every group the main switch fed onto its outlets, so all rails are fed from it), switching, control, accessories and measuring — and on a phone it is the whole palette. The green + under a group's last breaker adds a breaker too. Nothing asks a question: what the board checks doubt is listed under Board warnings under the drawing, and never blocks the design. Advanced wiring in the device panel lists the device's wires terminal by terminal, × removes one, and Connect joins one of its terminals to any other device's. Before the first change, Minimal, Standard and Max safety pick what the starting board has: the spare rail, DIN socket, contactor, always-on group and voltage relay.

5. Materials and prices

Bill of materials lists what to buy — cable per cross-section with your waste margin, conduit, boxes (a deep box for a dimmer, or a switch box where three or more cables meet), accessories, board equipment with its ferrules (a twin one where two links share a terminal) — and the labour. Default prices are planning estimates: replace them with yours. Quotation opens Documents, where the quotation is printed and sent.

Your suppliers' price lists

  1. Under Suppliers, Import a price list (CSV or Excel XLSX as your distributor sends it).
  2. Check the columns Elecivo guessed (code, name, price, unit, pack size), whether prices include VAT, and name the supplier.
  3. On a BOM line, the truck button lets you search all your catalogues and pick the item that supplies it. The catalogue remembers the match for every project.

Lines are priced from the first catalogue that has them; reorder catalogues to prefer one supplier. Prices are converted to net, per metre or piece, and the order rounds up to whole rolls and boxes. A price you type on a line always wins. Prices are copied into the project when applied, so a saved revision keeps the prices it quoted.

Breakers and RCDs on the BOM screen picks a manufacturer series (NOARK Ex9 for now). Each MCB, RCD and RCBO the series makes then shows its part number on the BOM, the supplier orders and the board legend. Lines the series has no part for stay generic. The series only names the part; prices still come from your catalogues, and a price list with the series' part numbers prices them without matching each line by hand.

Material on site

Under Material on site, on the Work log screen next to the site work, log deliveries as they arrive (and, for things the work log doesn't cover, what was used). Each line shows needed, delivered, used, on site and still to buy, by stage — rough-in (cable, conduit, boxes, the enclosure) or second fix (accessories and protection). Cable used comes from the metres pulled in the work log, accessories from the devices marked installed. Once deliveries are logged, the Bill of materials shows a To buy column too. In Documents, choose the stage under Orders for to order only what is still to buy.

6. Documents and your letterhead

Under As-built changes, on the Work log screen, record what was changed on site and differs from the design: the device, circuit, route or board, the room, and why. Give the date and who made the change. Each entry is saved at once, like the work log, so the crew can add one from the site. Once the design has been brought in line, pick the saved revision that shows the change under In design; the As-built changes document prints it (or Not yet). Remove an entry you entered by mistake.

Documents builds every document from a saved revision (or, marked DRAFT, from unsaved changes): quotation, plans, circuit schedule, cable journal, board, the test protocol to fill in on site, material lists, orders per supplier, labour, work log, as-built changes (what was built differently from the design). Preview any of them, download PDF, CSV or Excel, or a whole pack — client, site, engineering, procurement or everything.

Board labels and markers starts with a sheet of label strips, one per rail, at true size: each device's sticker is as wide as its modules (17.5 mm each) and carries a coloured band (lighting yellow, sockets green, appliances red, merged over neighbours that share it), an icon, the rooms the circuit feeds, its designation, circuit and rating. To change a sticker, select the device on the Board tab in Edit and open Label: pick the band colour and icon, type the band or the text; an empty field shows what the design gives. Print that page at 100 % on label stock, check it against the label carrier of your enclosure and cut along the lines; the cable and terminal markers follow on their own pages.

Once the quotation has gone to the client, mark it on the bill of materials: Mark the quote of r… as sent (from a saved revision), then Accepted or Rejected when they answer. The start screen shows where each project stands, and it is saved with the next revision.

Your letterhead

  1. Company profile…: company name, address, contact, VAT number, bank details, a footer line, your logo and an accent colour. Fill it in once.
  2. Apply company profile copies it into the open project; then save a revision.

Documents can be produced in English, Bulgarian, German or Russian, whatever language the app is in.

7. Account, sync and sharing

Open Account & sync (the cloud in the top bar) and create an account. We send a link to your e-mail address: open it to confirm the address. Until you do, you can work and sync your own projects, but nobody can share a project with you. Then:

  • Upload to the server puts the open project there; Sync now sends and fetches saved revisions. Only saved revisions sync.
  • Share a project by e-mail as Owner, Editor, Crew (logs site work only and sees only the Site screens) or Viewer. The person gets an e-mail; the project reaches them once they have an account with that address and have confirmed it. Until then it is listed as invited, and you can withdraw the invitation.
  • On another device, sign in and Download the project.
  • If two devices both saved, Elecivo merges them: what changed on one side is kept; where both changed the same thing, you choose which to keep, item by item (Merge and upload).
  • Your supplier catalogues and company profile follow your account to every device.

Your data

Export my data (ZIP) in Account & sync downloads everything your account has on the server: your account details, catalogues and company profile, every project you are a member of (each as a project file you can import again) and the drawings and logos they use.

Deleting your account

Delete account… at the bottom of Account & sync shows what would happen. Projects you alone own that others still work on need a new owner first (Make owner), or you delete them for everyone. Projects nobody else is in go with your account. In the projects that stay, your name in their history becomes "Former member". Your password confirms it. The projects on this device stay on it.

8. Site work from a phone

  1. Open the app on the phone once while online and sign in. Add it to the home screen from the browser's menu: it then opens like an app, even without a connection.
  2. Work log: pick the crew member (the phone remembers), the task, the quantity and the hours, tick the devices fitted or tested, and Log work.
  3. Each entry is saved at once. Offline, it waits on the phone and goes to the server by itself when the connection returns.

The plan then shows each device as planned, installed or tested, and the Work log compares hours spent with the planned labour.

Test readings

Under Tests on the Work log, enter who tested, the instrument and its calibration, and the Ze measured at the origin; then each circuit's readings — insulation, R1 + R2, Zs, the RCD trip time and current, polarity. Each value is checked as you type against what it must be (shown in the empty field), and Save records the circuit at once, offline too. A later test of a circuit replaces the earlier one. A measured Zs replaces the calculated one in the checks, a measured Ze the stated one, and the test protocol in Documents prints the readings and their result.

9. When something is off

The cloud button has a dot

Something needs your decision — usually a design revision to upload, or both devices changed the same thing. Open Account & sync.

"The drawing's image is not on this device"

The plan knows where the drawing lies but this device has no copy, and the server doesn't either (the device that loaded it hasn't synced yet). Sync there, or load the drawing again: the same file keeps its calibration.

A catalogue was "not applied"

Its currency isn't the project's. Elecivo doesn't convert currencies.

Forgot the password

Forgot your password? in the sign-in form sends a link valid for an hour. Using it signs out every other device.

An update is ready

A "new version" prompt appears; Reload when it suits you. Nothing is lost: unsaved changes stay in the browser.

10. Feedback

Use Send feedback at the bottom of the menu (where your installation offers it). It names the app version and the project you had open, never its content. Screenshots and the steps that led to a problem help most.
